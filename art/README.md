# Art

Two sources, one binding layer.

| what | where | licence |
|---|---|---|
| Terrain, buildings, people (259 PNGs) | `kenney_medieval-rts/` | **CC0** — Kenney Vleugels, [kenney.nl](https://kenney.nl) |
| Vehicles, goods, action glyphs (79 SVGs) | `art/generated/` | drawn here, same terms |
| Which sprite goes with what | `convoy/sprites.py` | — |

Kenney's pack is CC0: usable in personal and commercial projects, credit
appreciated but not required. It is credited here and in the rendered map.

## Regenerating

```bash
python3 art/make_art.py        # redraws art/generated/**
python3 -m convoy.sprites      # asserts every entity in data.py has art
python3 render_world.py        # newest run -> world.html
```

`art/generated/` is committed. It is deterministic output, but it is also what
the renderer reads at build time, and a classroom machine should not need a
working `make_art.py` to open a map.

## What was missing, and what was done about it

The pack has no vehicles and no inventory icons. Convoy has six vehicles and 63
tradeable goods.

**These were drawn as SVG, not generated by an image model.** Generated raster
art does not match a flat-shaded CC0 sprite pack — the outlines, the palette and
the projection would all drift, and the result reads as two games stapled
together. Instead `palette.py` holds colours sampled straight out of the pack's
own PNGs:

```
for every opaque pixel in PNG/Default size/**/*.png -> Counter(hex)
```

898 distinct colours; the top ~25 carry the pack and are what `palette.py`
records. Everything in `make_art.py` follows the three rules read off those same
sprites: flat fills, outline in a **darker shade of the fill** rather than
black, one lighter highlight plane.

Goods are drawn as a taxonomy, not as 63 unrelated objects — one shape per
category, tinted per material. Ore is ore whether copper, tin or iron; a cuirass
is a cuirass in bronze, iron or leather. That is how the pack itself gets 58
tiles out of about eight ideas, and it means a good added to `data.py` inherits
sane art instead of shipping as a blank square.

## The unit grid — do not "tidy" this

The pack's 24 people are **4 faction colours × 6 poses**, and the colours do not
start where the filenames do. Blue begins at 23 and wraps past 24 to 1.
Determined by counting pixels, not by eye:

```
blue #1ea7e1 -> 23    red #e27952 -> 5    green #1b914d -> 11    grey #acb8b8 -> 17
```

`sprites.unit(faction, pose)` indexes that cycle. Flattening it to a naive 0–23
repaints every agent the wrong colour, silently — `tests/test_sprites.py` pins
it.
